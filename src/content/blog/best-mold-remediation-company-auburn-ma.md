## What's the difference between mold remediation and mold removal?

Mold remediation means reducing mold to normal, healthy levels and fixing the moisture source that let it grow, not eliminating every spore, which is biologically impossible since mold spores exist in outdoor air everywhere. "Mold removal" is a marketing term, not an industry standard; a contractor who promises to "remove all mold" is either overselling or doesn't understand that mold spores will always be present at background levels.

The actual work in a remediation job includes containment with poly sheeting and negative air pressure, HEPA air scrubbing, removal of mold-affected porous materials like drywall and insulation that can't be cleaned, and HEPA vacuuming of remaining surfaces. The job isn't done until the moisture source, whether it's a roof leak, a crawl space with no vapor barrier, or a bathroom fan that vents into the attic, is also corrected. The [EPA's mold remediation guidance](https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-remediation-schools-and-commercial-buildings-guide-chapter-1) frames this the same way: fix the water problem first, or the mold comes back regardless of how thorough the cleanup was.

## What should you ask a mold remediation contractor before hiring them?

Ask these four questions before signing anything, and treat vague or evasive answers as a warning sign:

1. **How will the work area be contained?** Plastic sheeting and negative air pressure should isolate the work zone so spores don't spread to unaffected rooms during demolition.
2. **What equipment runs during the job?** HEPA air scrubbers and HEPA vacuums should be standard, not optional add-ons.
3. **What happens to affected porous materials?** Drywall, insulation, and carpet padding with mold growth generally need to be removed and replaced, not just cleaned.
4. **Is clearance testing done by someone outside your crew?** A third-party, independent air or surface test after remediation confirms the job actually worked, rather than taking the remediation company's own word for it.

If the mold is tied to a leak, add a fifth question: who fixes the moisture source, and is that included in this scope or a separate bid?

## Do you need independent mold testing after remediation?

Yes, independent clearance testing from a third party not affiliated with the remediation company is the only objective way to confirm a mold job actually passed. A remediation company testing its own work has an obvious conflict of interest, even when the crew did everything right.

Clearance testing typically involves air sampling and sometimes surface swabs, compared against outdoor baseline readings, with results reviewed by an independent hygienist or testing lab. This step matters most on larger jobs, insurance-involved claims, or real estate transactions where a clean bill of health needs to hold up to scrutiny. For smaller, contained jobs, a visual follow-up and moisture-meter readings may be sufficient, but ask your contractor directly which approach they recommend and why.
