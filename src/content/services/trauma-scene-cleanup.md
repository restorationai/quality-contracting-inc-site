## What Trauma Scene Cleanup actually involves

Trauma scene cleanup covers the removal and decontamination of blood, bodily fluids, and other biological matter following a death, suicide, violent crime, or serious accident. The work goes beyond visible cleanup: affected flooring, subflooring, drywall, and porous materials often need to be removed because bloodborne pathogens can penetrate surfaces that look clean on top. Crews work in full personal protective equipment, use hospital-grade disinfectants and enzymatic cleaners, and dispose of contaminated materials through a regulated biohazard waste process rather than a standard dumpster. Timelines vary by scene size and what materials are affected, but most single-room jobs are resolved within one to three days once access is confirmed.

## Our process

1. **Scene assessment and containment** - technicians evaluate the affected area, identify contamination boundaries, and seal off the space to prevent cross-contact with unaffected parts of the property.
2. **PPE staging and biohazard removal** - crews suit up in protective equipment and remove visible biological material, saturated materials, and any items that cannot be safely disinfected.
3. **Enzymatic and hospital-grade disinfection** - remaining surfaces are treated with enzymatic cleaners designed to break down biological residue, followed by disinfection of walls, flooring, and fixtures.
4. **Odor neutralization** - affected air and surfaces are treated to address odor caused by biological breakdown, which can linger even after visible cleanup is complete.
5. **Disposal and documentation** - contaminated waste is packaged and transported according to biohazard handling protocols, with photo documentation and a waste manifest provided for the property owner's and insurer's records.

## What separates a good trauma cleanup response from a bad one

The most common mistake in trauma scene cleaning is treating it like a deep clean rather than a decontamination job. Surface cleaning that leaves subflooring, grout lines, or baseboards untreated allows bloodborne pathogens to remain even when a room looks and smells clean. A thorough response identifies every material the contamination reached, not just what's visible, and removes porous materials that cannot be fully disinfected rather than attempting to salvage them. Insurance adjusters reviewing these claims typically want clear photo documentation of the affected area before and after cleanup, an itemized list of materials removed, and a waste disposal manifest showing the biohazard material was handled through a proper channel, not placed in regular trash. Skipping that paperwork is one of the fastest ways a claim gets delayed or denied.
