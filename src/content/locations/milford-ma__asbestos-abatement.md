## Why Milford Properties See Asbestos Issues

Milford grew up around granite quarrying and textile manufacturing, and a lot of the housing built to support that workforce, from early 20th-century triple-deckers to small single-family homes near the downtown core, used materials that were standard practice at the time: asbestos-wrapped steam pipes, vermiculite insulation, vinyl floor tile with asbestos backing, and textured "popcorn" ceiling finishes applied well into the 1980s. When these properties change hands or get renovated, contractors frequently uncover these materials mid-project. Worcester County's older building stock, combined with a steady pace of basement finishing and kitchen remodels in town, means asbestos discovery during routine renovation is a recurring pattern here, not a rare surprise.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Milford

We start with sampling, not guesswork. A material that looks like ordinary ceiling texture or floor tile gets lab-tested before anyone touches it, because visual inspection alone can't confirm asbestos content. If results come back positive, we isolate the work area with poly sheeting and negative air pressure, set up a decontamination zone, and remove the material following wet-removal methods that keep fibers from becoming airborne. Massachusetts requires state notification before certain abatement projects begin, and we handle that filing as part of the job rather than leaving it to the homeowner. Debris is double-bagged, labeled, and disposed of through a licensed asbestos waste facility, consistent with EPA NESHAP handling requirements. Once removal is complete, we clear the space before anyone resumes work.

## Local note

A detail that catches a lot of Milford homeowners off guard: vermiculite insulation in older attics here is frequently mixed in with blown-in fiberglass from a later upgrade, so a quick peek into the attic hatch doesn't tell you much. We've found that testing a sample from several spots in the attic, not just near the hatch, gives a more accurate read, because vermiculite distribution in these older homes is rarely uniform across the whole space.
