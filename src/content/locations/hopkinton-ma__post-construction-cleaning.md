Sawdust in the HVAC returns, a film of joint compound on every windowsill, paint overspray on fixtures you weren't told got painted: that's the state most Hopkinton properties are in the day a renovation or new build wraps up. Between the subdivisions that have gone up across town over the past two decades and the steady stream of kitchen and basement remodels in Hopkinton's older colonial-era homes, post-construction and specialty cleaning is rarely optional here. It's the step that turns a finished job site into a livable house.

## Why Hopkinton Renovations Leave So Much Behind

Hopkinton's housing stock is a genuine mix: center-of-town homes built decades ago alongside subdivisions that filled in as the town grew into a MetroWest commuter community. Each brings its own cleanup problem. Older homes along and near Route 135 often have forced-hot-water heat and plaster walls, so renovation dust settles into baseboard heaters and radiator fins instead of blowing through ductwork, which means it has to be wiped out by hand rather than vacuumed through a vent. Newer subdivision homes, by contrast, usually have full HVAC systems that spread fine drywall and sanding dust through every supply register in the house during a remodel, so the cleanup has to include duct and vent work, not just visible surfaces. Either way, the dust settles for weeks if it isn't addressed right after the trades leave.

## Our Post-Construction and Specialty Cleaning Process

We start with a dry pass: vacuuming framing dust, drywall grit, and debris out of corners, window tracks, and closet shelving before anything gets wet, since wiping dry dust with a damp cloth just turns it into paste. From there we move to fixtures, cabinetry, and appliances, degreasing adhesive residue and pulling painter's tape overspray off glass and hardware. Floors get a final pass calibrated to the material, dry dust mopping before wet cleaning on hardwood, versus a more aggressive wet extraction on tile and grout lines where grout haze tends to cling. Last comes detail work: light fixtures, vent covers, baseboards, and any construction film still on windows or appliances. On occupied renovations, we coordinate timing with the builder or general contractor so the clean doesn't happen before the last coat of paint has fully cured.

## Local Note

One thing that catches builders off guard in Hopkinton: homes on well water, which is common outside the main water district, often show mineral deposits on fixtures and glass from construction-phase water use that municipal-water homes don't get as badly. A standard glass cleaner won't cut hard-water film the way it would in town-center homes on municipal service, so we adjust the fixture and glass pass with a different solution on well-water properties to avoid leaving a cloudy residue behind.
