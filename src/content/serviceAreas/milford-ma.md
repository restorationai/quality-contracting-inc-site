## Restoration emergencies common in Milford

Milford's housing stock and its location along the Charles River and Mill River watershed combine to create a particular set of restoration calls. Heavy rain events common to central Massachusetts push groundwater and surface runoff toward low-lying parcels near the rivers, and older storm drainage in parts of town was not designed for the volume that modern storms deliver. Add in a New England freeze-thaw cycle that stresses roofing, siding, and foundation joints every winter, and you get a steady mix of basement seepage, ice-dam related roof leaks, and frozen or burst supply lines in homes that sit empty or under-heated during a cold snap. Granite-era construction downtown and the wood-frame triple-deckers and single-families built up through the early twentieth century each bring their own failure points, from fieldstone foundations that were never meant to be fully dry to original plumbing runs well past their intended service life.
