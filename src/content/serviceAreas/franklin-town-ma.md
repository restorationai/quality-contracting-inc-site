## Restoration emergencies common in Franklin Town

Franklin Town sits in Norfolk County, where New England's freeze-thaw cycle puts real stress on older plumbing and foundations every winter. Pipes that run through unheated crawlspaces or exterior walls are the usual source of midwinter burst-pipe calls, and the thaw that follows a hard freeze often reveals damage that happened days earlier, hidden behind plaster or under flooring. Spring and early summer bring heavier rain events that test grading and sump systems, especially on lots with clay-heavy or poorly draining soil common across this part of the county. Nor'easters and the occasional remnant of a tropical system can also drive wind-driven rain into roofing and siding that wasn't built for sustained gusts, which is why we see a steady mix of water intrusion and wind damage work tied directly to the regional weather pattern rather than any single storm type.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Franklin Town

Franklin Town's housing stock spans several distinct eras: colonial and Victorian-period homes near the older parts of town, a substantial wave of ranch and colonial-style construction from the 1960s through 1980s, and newer subdivisions built from the 1990s onward. Older homes more often carry galvanized supply lines, cast iron drains, and in some cases knob-and-tube wiring, all of which affect how a restoration crew plans demolition and whether asbestos-containing materials or lead paint need to be addressed before work proceeds. Basements are common across the housing stock rather than slab-on-grade construction, which means groundwater and foundation seepage are a recurring factor in water losses here. Norfolk County soils tend toward clay and glacial till with pockets of ledge, conditions that slow drainage away from foundations after heavy rain. Structural repairs and rebuild work typically require a permit through the Franklin Town building department, filed under the Massachusetts State Building Code, and any scope touching electrical or plumbing systems usually needs its own trade permit alongside the general building permit.
