## Why North Brookfield Properties See Water Damage

North Brookfield's housing stock skews older than much of the state, with a mix of 19th-century farmhouses, converted mill-era homes, and ranch-style houses built on fieldstone or early poured foundations that were never engineered for today's heavier storm volumes. Those foundations tend to be more porous than modern concrete, so basements here often take on water through old mortar joints and sill plates rather than a single dramatic crack. Add in New England's freeze-thaw winters, where pipes in unheated additions or farmhouse ells can split during a January cold snap and not get discovered until a thaw, and you have a town where slow, hidden water damage is just as common as a sudden burst pipe. Many properties also rely on private wells and septic systems, which changes how we think about contamination risk when groundwater or septic backup is involved in a loss.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in North Brookfield

The process starts the same way it would anywhere: stop the source, extract standing water, and assess what's wet beneath the surface. In North Brookfield's older homes, that assessment takes longer because plaster-and-lath walls and wide-board subflooring hold moisture differently than drywall and plywood do in newer construction. We check fieldstone or rubble foundation walls for wicking, since water can travel laterally through old mortar for days after a storm has passed. Structural drying equipment gets placed based on what the moisture meter tells us about each material, not a one-size-fits-all setup, because a farmhouse with balloon framing dries differently than a 1970s ranch. Once the structure reads dry and stable, we document everything for the insurance file before any reconstruction begins.

## Local note

A lot of North Brookfield's older farmhouses were built with the main structure and a later-added ell or barn connection, and that seam is often where the insulation and plumbing are weakest. When a pipe freezes and splits in one of those additions, the water frequently travels along the old sill connection into the main house basement before anyone notices a problem, which means the visible damage point and the actual source can be two different rooms. We've learned to check that transition area first on any older North Brookfield property, even when the obvious wet spot points somewhere else.
