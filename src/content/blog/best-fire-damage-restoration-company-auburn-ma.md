## What Questions Should You Ask a Fire Restoration Contractor Before You Sign?

Before signing a work authorization, ask for a written scope of work, confirm whether the company bills your insurer directly, and ask who performs the reconstruction once mitigation is complete.

Five questions worth asking every contractor on your short list:

1. Will you give me a written scope of work before any demolition starts?
2. Do you bill my insurance company directly, or do I pay out of pocket and get reimbursed?
3. Who handles the structural repairs and drywall after mitigation, your own crew or a sub I have to find myself?
4. How do you document contents, furniture, electronics, keepsakes, before cleaning or disposing of anything?
5. What's your plan for [smoke odor](/services/smoke-damage-restoration/) if it reached the HVAC system or traveled to rooms the fire never touched?

A contractor who can't answer the HVAC and odor question clearly is a red flag. Smoke particles travel through ductwork into rooms far from the fire, and odor that isn't addressed at the source tends to come back after a few humid days.

## How Do You Work With Your Insurance Adjuster on a Fire Claim?

Document the damage with photos before any cleanup starts, keep the restoration estimate separate from the reconstruction estimate, and let the restoration company's project manager communicate scope directly with your adjuster.

Most homeowners policies treat fire as a covered peril, and [standard homeowners coverage](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance) typically pays for sudden fire damage along with smoke and soot cleanup tied to that loss, but coverage details vary by carrier and policy, so confirm specifics with your agent before assuming anything is included. You have a duty to mitigate further damage once it's safe to do so, which is part of why fast board-up and drying matters for the claim itself, not just the property.

Keep the mitigation invoice (emergency services, drying, odor treatment) separate from the reconstruction estimate (drywall, flooring, trim, paint). Adjusters process these as different line items, and a contractor who blends them into one number makes your claim harder to approve. For more on documenting and [settling an insurance claim after a disaster](https://www.iii.org/article/settling-insurance-claims-after-a-disaster), keep every receipt and photograph the damage before anything is moved or discarded.
