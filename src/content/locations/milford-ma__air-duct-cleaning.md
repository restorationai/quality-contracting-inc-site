Milford's older triple-deckers and early 1900s mill-era homes were built for radiators and gravity furnaces, not sealed duct systems, so when owners retrofit forced-air heating and cooling, the ductwork often gets routed through tight joist bays, old chimney chases, and crawlspaces never designed for it. Dust, insulation fibers, and rodent debris collect in those cramped runs fast, and homeowners usually notice it first as a musty smell when the furnace kicks on or a fine layer of dust that resettles no matter how often they clean.

## Why Milford Homes See Duct Buildup

Worcester County's humid summers and damp springs push moisture into basements and crawlspaces throughout the year, and that moisture finds its way into supply and return ducts, especially in homes where the system was added decades after construction. Milford's mix of early 1900s mill-worker housing and mid-century ranch construction means duct layouts vary house to house. Some runs sit in unconditioned attics that bake in summer and freeze in winter, which stresses seams and lets outside dust and insulation particles pull into the system. Homes near Milford's older industrial corridors sometimes still have original sheet metal trunk lines with soldered joints that have loosened over the years, creating gaps where debris and pests get in.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process

We start by inspecting the full system, supply runs, returns, the plenum, and the dryer vent if it's part of the job, using a camera where access is tight. Each register gets sealed and the trunk line is put under negative pressure with a HEPA-filtered vacuum collector so loosened debris pulls out of the house instead of blowing into the living space. Technicians then agitate the interior of the ducts with rotary brushes or air whips sized to the duct diameter, working section by section from the furthest supply runs back to the air handler. For homes with visible mold staining or persistent odor, we apply an EPA-registered antimicrobial sanitizer rated for HVAC use as a final step, and dryer vents get cleared separately since lint buildup there is a fire risk the duct system itself doesn't carry.

## Local note

A lot of Milford's older housing stock still has the original basement bulkhead and stone or brick foundation from the mill-town era, and ductwork run through those foundations often has gaps around the sill plate where it passes from the basement into the first floor. Those gaps let in basement dust, old insulation fragments, and sometimes evidence of rodent activity, which is why a full inspection before cleaning matters more in Milford's older homes than it does in newer subdivisions where duct runs are factory-sealed.
