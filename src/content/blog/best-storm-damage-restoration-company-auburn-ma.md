## What should you check before hiring any storm damage contractor in Worcester County?

Check four things before you sign anything: licensing, insurance documentation practices, in-house tarping, and whether the same company does the rebuild. Skipping any one of these is how homeowners end up with a patched roof, a denied claim line item, or a six-month gap waiting on a second contractor.

Massachusetts requires contractors doing home improvement work to carry a Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration, and structural work typically requires a Construction Supervisor License (CSL) as well. Ask any contractor you're considering, including this one, for their HIC registration number before signing a contract, and verify it directly through the state's [Home Improvement Contractor Program](https://www.mass.gov/home-improvement-contractor-program) rather than taking a company's word for it.

Insurance documentation is the other make-or-break factor. A storm claim lives or dies on photos of the damage before tarping, a written scope of the emergency work, and a clear line between mitigation (stopping further damage) and the rebuild (returning the structure to pre-loss condition). Ask how a company documents that handoff for the adjuster, because a poorly documented scope is a common reason storm claims get partially denied.

## Does homeowners insurance cover storm damage in Massachusetts?

Most standard homeowners policies cover sudden storm damage like wind-driven roof damage, a tree limb through the roof, or hail impact, but they typically exclude flood water from rising groundwater or overflowing waterways, which needs a separate flood policy. The [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/which-disasters-are-covered-by-homeowners-insurance) breaks this down by peril, and it's worth reading before you assume a basement full of storm water is automatically covered the same way a torn-off roof section is.

Water that enters through a storm-damaged roof generally starts as relatively clean water, but it degrades quickly once it's running through insulation, drywall, and framing, which is part of why fast tarping matters for both the structure and the claim. Once the source is stopped, affected materials are typically dried or removed following the [ANSI/IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/) for water damage restoration, regardless of which company is doing the drying.
