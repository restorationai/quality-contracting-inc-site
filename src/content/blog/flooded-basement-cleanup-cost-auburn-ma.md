## What Does Flooded Basement Cleanup Cost in Auburn, MA?

Flooded basement cleanup in Auburn typically runs $1,000 to $3,000 for a small unfinished space with clean water and no rebuild, and climbs past $10,000 when a finished basement floods with sewage or groundwater and needs full demolition. ## What Drives the Price: Water Category, Square Footage, and Finished vs. Unfinished?

Three things move the price more than anything else: how contaminated the water was, how many square feet got wet, and whether the basement was finished living space or bare concrete and studs.

Water gets sorted into categories under the [ANSI/IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/). Category 1 is clean water, like a burst supply line. Category 2 is gray water with real contamination, such as groundwater seeping in under hydrostatic pressure. Category 3 is grossly contaminated black water, including any sewer backup or rising flood water. Clean water sitting on bare concrete can often be extracted and dried without removing anything; contaminated water almost always means something gets torn out.

Square footage sets the extraction and equipment time. A typical basement needs three to five days of air movers and a dehumidifier running to reach safe moisture levels, longer if the slab or fieldstone walls are holding water, since masonry falls into the slower Class 4 drying category under the S500 standard. More square footage means more equipment and more days of rental and monitoring, which shows up directly in the invoice.

Finished versus unfinished is the biggest single swing. In a finished basement, wet carpet and pad almost always come out, since padding holds water against the subfloor and can't be dried in place. Drywall gets cut and removed starting about 12 to 24 inches above the waterline, a "flood cut," whenever the water was Category 2 or 3, because contaminated water wicks upward inside the wall cavity. An unfinished basement with bare concrete skips most of that demolition, which is why the same flood can cost three times as much next door in a finished rec room.

## Why Does a Sewage Backup Cost More to Clean Up?

A sewage backup costs more because it's automatically Category 3 water, which means contaminated porous materials get removed instead of dried, and the crew needs PPE and antimicrobial treatment the job wouldn't otherwise require.

Any backup from a floor drain, toilet, or sewer line beyond the trap counts as black water, regardless of how it looks. That means carpet, pad, baseboards, and often the lower section of drywall all come out rather than getting dried in place, and the subfloor and framing need disinfection before anything gets rebuilt. [Sewage cleanup](/services/sewage-cleanup/) work also requires respirators and protective suits, which adds labor cost on top of the extra demolition. Skipping that step to save money is how a basement ends up with a hidden odor problem months later.

## Does Insurance Cover Flooded Basement Cleanup?

Most homeowners policies cover a flooded basement when the water came from a sudden, accidental source inside the home, like a burst pipe or a failed water heater, but they typically exclude outside flood water and exclude sewer backup or sump pump failure unless you've added a water backup endorsement.

Groundwater and surface flooding generally fall under flood insurance, not a standard homeowners policy; that coverage comes through the [National Flood Insurance Program](https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance) or a private flood policy, not your regular carrier. If a backup or a failed sump pump caused the flood, check your declarations page for a water backup endorsement specifically, since it's the only thing that typically pays for that scenario. Either way, document everything with photos before cleanup starts and keep every receipt; our [full guide to homeowners insurance and water damage](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/) walks through what adjusters actually ask for.
