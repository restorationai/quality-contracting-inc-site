## Why Bellingham Homes Build Up Duct Contaminants Faster

Bellingham sits in a part of Massachusetts that swings between sticky, humid summers and long, dry heating seasons, and that combination is rough on ductwork. Summer humidity lets dust bind together and cling to duct interiors instead of blowing through, while the dry heat of a Massachusetts winter kicks whatever's loose back into circulation. A lot of the housing stock in and around Bellingham dates to the town's growth as a bedroom community from the 1960s through the 1980s, which means plenty of homes are running original or early-generation ductwork that was never designed with filtration standards in mind. Add in wood heat as a supplemental source in some older properties, and you get soot and fine ash finding their way into the same supply trunks as the furnace air.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Bellingham

We start with an inspection of the full system, supply and return, before any equipment touches the ductwork. That matters in this area because a fair number of Bellingham homes have additions or finished basements where ductwork was extended or rerouted over the years, sometimes without matching the original sizing or material. We isolate each run, use negative-pressure vacuum equipment to pull contaminants out of the trunk and branch lines, and agitate buildup along the interior walls so it doesn't just get pushed further down the line. Registers and grilles get pulled and cleaned separately. If your system includes a dryer vent run through an exterior wall, which is common in the ranch and split-level homes built here in the 70s and 80s, we check that line too since lint buildup in those runs is a fire risk independent of your HVAC ducts.
