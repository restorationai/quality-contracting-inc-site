## Why Upton Properties See Water Damage Issues

Upton sits in Worcester County, where winters bring hard freezes followed by rapid thaws, the exact cycle that cracks supply lines and splits hose bibs in homes that weren't built with modern insulation standards. A lot of the housing stock here predates current plumbing codes, meaning galvanized or early-generation copper lines are reaching the end of their service life right about now. Add in the number of properties on private wells and septic systems common to this part of the county, and a failed well pressure tank or a backed-up line can introduce water into a basement in ways that town-sewer neighborhoods rarely deal with. Heavy spring rain and snowmelt also push groundwater up against foundations on the lower-lying lots, which is where we see slow seepage turn into standing water in finished basements.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Upton

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mounted or portable units depending on how much access the property allows, since some older Upton driveways and setbacks don't accommodate a full-size truck close to the house. From there we assess what's actually wet, not just what looks wet. Plaster and old lath in pre-code homes hold moisture differently than drywall does, so moisture mapping with a meter matters more here than it would in a newer subdivision. Structural drying follows, using air movers and dehumidification calibrated to the room's materials and the season, because a damp basement in January dries on a different timeline than one in July. We document every reading throughout the job, which matters later if you're filing a claim.
