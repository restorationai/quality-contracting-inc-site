## Why Rutland Properties See Water Damage Issues

A lot of Rutland's housing stock predates modern plumbing codes, with additions and outbuildings tacked on over decades. Fieldstone and rubble foundations, common on older New England farmhouses, don't seal the way poured concrete does, so groundwater and snowmelt find their way in through mortar joints rather than a single obvious crack. On the newer side, homes on private wells often route pressure tanks and shutoffs into unheated basements or crawlspaces, and a hard freeze can split a fitting fast. Because the town is spread out with larger lots and longer driveways than a denser suburb, a slow leak behind a wall or under a slab can run for days before anyone notices, which is exactly the window mold needs to start colonizing wet drywall and framing.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Rutland

We start by identifying the water source and shutting it off, then move into extraction with truck-mounted or portable pumps depending on how the property is accessed. Rutland's rural lots mean we're often staging equipment farther from the house than in a tight neighborhood, so we plan hose runs and power needs before we start pulling water. Once standing water is out, we check moisture levels in subflooring, framing, and any fieldstone foundation walls, since stone and old mortar hold moisture longer than poured concrete and release it slowly even after the surface reads dry. Drying follows the IICRC S500 structural drying standard, with air movers and dehumidifiers positioned to pull moisture out of those slower-releasing materials rather than just the drywall surface. We document moisture readings throughout so there's a clear record for your insurance carrier if you're filing a claim.
