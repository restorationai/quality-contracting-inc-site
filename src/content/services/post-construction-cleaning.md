## What Post-Construction and Specialty Cleaning actually involves

Drywall dust gets into everything. It settles into HVAC returns, collects on window tracks, films over cabinet interiors, and hides in light fixture housings long after the last subcontractor has packed up. A general cleaning crew with mops and all-purpose spray cannot touch it, and a homeowner trying to tackle it with a household vacuum usually just redistributes fine particulate back into the air. Post-construction cleaning is a separate trade from routine cleaning: it starts with debris and dust removal using HEPA-filtered vacuums, moves through detail work on fixtures, glass, and cabinetry, and finishes with a final clean that gets a space ready to actually live in or hand over to a buyer or tenant.

The timeline depends on scope. A single remodeled bathroom might take a few hours. A whole-house renovation or new construction final clean, with trade debris still in rooms, protective floor coatings to pull up, and paint overspray on fixtures, is typically a multi-day job that moves room by room so dust from one area doesn't recontaminate a space that's already been detailed. Equipment includes HEPA vacuums, microfiber systems that don't scratch new finishes, non-abrasive adhesive and paint-overspray removers for glass and fixtures, and extension tools for high ceilings, fans, and light fixtures that general contractors rarely get to.

## Our process

1. **Walkthrough and scope.** We assess the space room by room, noting trade debris, dust load, adhesive residue, paint overspray, and any surfaces that need special handling (natural stone, new hardwood, factory-finish cabinetry).
2. **Debris and bulk removal.** Leftover construction material, packaging, protective floor paper, and tape get pulled and hauled before any detail cleaning starts.
3. **Dust abatement.** HEPA vacuuming of all surfaces, vents, and window tracks, followed by damp wiping of walls, trim, and ceilings where dust has settled into texture or paint.
4. **Detail and fixture clean.** Cabinet interiors and exteriors, light fixtures, plumbing fixtures, mirrors, and glass get hand-detailed to remove labels, adhesive, caulk haze, and overspray.
5. **Final clean and walkthrough.** Floors, baseboards, and remaining surfaces get a final pass, and we walk the space with you or the general contractor before it's considered move-in or close-ready.

## What separates a good post-construction clean from a bad one

The most common shortcut is skipping HVAC returns and supply vents. If dust gets pulled back into the system during the first weeks of occupancy, it recirculates through the house for months, which is why a thorough clean includes vent covers and visible ductwork, not just floors and counters. Another frequent miss is caulk haze and adhesive residue left on new glass or tile, both of which are easy to scratch if the wrong tool or chemical is used, which is why non-abrasive, surface-appropriate products matter more here than in routine house cleaning.

A good crew also sequences the work correctly: dusting and vacuuming happen before wet wiping, and high surfaces get cleaned before floors, so debris doesn't fall onto a surface that's already finished. Builders and project managers who are coordinating a closing date or tenant move-in also look for a crew that communicates realistic timelines rather than compressing a multi-day job into a few hours and leaving a final clean that looks rushed under good lighting.

## What does Post-Construction and Specialty Cleaning cost?

Costs vary with square footage, how much trade debris is left on-site, and how much detail work the finishes require (stone, factory cabinetry, and multi-story spaces all add time). Every project is different, and Quality Contracting, Inc. provides a written scope before work begins so there are no surprises on the invoice. Homeowners insurance typically does not cover routine post-construction cleaning, since it's considered part of the renovation or build cost rather than a loss, though cleanup tied directly to a covered event like fire or water damage repair may be billed differently.
