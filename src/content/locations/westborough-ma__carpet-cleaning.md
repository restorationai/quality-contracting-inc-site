## Why Westborough Carpets Take a Beating

Westborough's winters mean months of salt, sand, and melted snow getting carried in on boots before anyone thinks to take them off. That grit works down into the carpet backing and acts like sandpaper on fibers every time someone walks across the room, which is why entryway and hallway carpet in Westborough homes often wears out faster than the carpet five feet further in. Come summer, the region's humidity settles into finished basements and lower-level family rooms, and carpet there can hold onto moisture and a faint mildew smell even when it looks and feels dry on top. Add in a housing stock that ranges from original wall-to-wall carpet in decades-old colonials to newer builds with denser synthetic pile, and no two Westborough carpet jobs call for quite the same settings on the equipment.

## Our Carpet Cleaning Process in Westborough

We start with a walk-through to identify fiber type, pile height, and where the heaviest soil and staining sit, since salt and sand near an entry need a different pre-treatment than a wine stain in a dining room. A dry pass with a commercial-grade vacuum pulls out loose grit before any water touches the carpet, because wet sand just turns to paste and grinds into fibers instead of lifting out. From there we apply a pre-conditioning solution matched to the soil type, agitate it into the pile, and follow with hot water extraction performed to the IICRC S100 carpet cleaning standard, which sets the benchmark for water temperature, dwell time, and extraction passes. Stains get individual attention with targeted spotting agents rather than a blanket treatment, and we finish by grooming the pile so it dries evenly instead of matting down in traffic patterns.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Carpet

For residential jobs we lean on truck-mounted hot water extraction, which pulls more moisture and embedded soil out of the pile than a portable unit can manage, especially useful against the sand and road grime Westborough winters leave behind. Commercial spaces with low-pile carpet tile, like lobbies and conference rooms in the town's office parks, often respond better to encapsulation cleaning, which uses less water and lets the space go back into service faster. Pet stains and odor get an enzyme-based treatment that breaks down the organic matter rather than just masking it, and for carpet that's picked up a musty basement smell we add a sanitization step aimed at the bacteria and mold spores living in the fibers, not just the surface soil.
