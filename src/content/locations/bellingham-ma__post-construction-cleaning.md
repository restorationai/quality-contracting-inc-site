**Dust after a renovation doesn't stay where the contractor leaves it.** Drywall powder, adhesive overspray, and sawdust work their way into vents, baseboards, and window tracks long after the last subcontractor packs up. In Bellingham, where renovation permits cover everything from older farmhouses near the town center to newer builds along the I-495 corridor, that final clean has to account for very different kinds of residue depending on the house. Post-construction and specialty cleaning closes the gap between "done building" and "ready to live in."

## Why Bellingham Renovations Leave Behind More Than Dust

Bellingham's housing stock is a mix: older homes closer to town that have gone through additions and kitchen gut jobs, and newer construction near the 495 interchange where whole-house builds and basement finishing are common. Each type leaves a different mess. Older homes often have plaster dust mixed with decades of settled residue disturbed during demo, which tends to be finer and more stubborn in HVAC returns than drywall dust from new construction. Newer builds generate more adhesive, caulk, and finish-coat overspray on windows and fixtures. Either way, fine particulate settles for days after a crew leaves, which is why a final clean done too early just means doing it again.

## Our Post-Construction and Specialty Cleaning Process

We start top to bottom: light fixtures, crown molding, and cabinet tops first, since dust falls. HEPA vacuuming handles vents, returns, and baseboards before any wet wiping starts, so we're not just smearing fine particulate across a surface. Window tracks and sills get detailed separately, since construction grit there scratches glass if it's wiped instead of lifted out. Adhesive residue, paint overspray on hardware, and caulk smears on tile or glass get spot-treated rather than scrubbed broadly, which protects finishes that are often still curing. The last pass is floors, done after everything above them is clean, so debris isn't resettling on a surface we already finished.

## Local note

A good number of Bellingham properties, particularly outside the areas served by municipal water, run on private wells. That matters for post-construction cleaning because well water leaves mineral scale on new fixtures, tile, and glass shower doors that's chemically different from the chlorination residue you'd fight in a home on town water. We adjust the cleaning solution accordingly on well-water properties instead of using a standard glass-and-chrome approach that can actually haze hard-water deposits rather than lift them.
