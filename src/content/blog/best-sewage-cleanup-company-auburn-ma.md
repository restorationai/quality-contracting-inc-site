## What Makes a Sewage Cleanup Company Different From a Plumber or Carpet Cleaner?

A sewage cleanup company is equipped and trained to handle Category 3 water, the IICRC's term for grossly contaminated water that includes sewage, toilet overflow beyond the trap, and backups from floor drains. A plumber fixes the pipe; a carpet cleaner shampoos surfaces. Neither removes contaminated porous materials or disinfects a structure to a recognized standard.

Under the [ANSI/IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/), Category 3 water is treated differently than a clean supply-line leak. Porous materials that absorbed the water, drywall, carpet pad, insulation, often get removed rather than dried in place, because drying alone doesn't neutralize what's in the water. A plumber's job ends when the clog clears. A carpet cleaner's equipment isn't built to extract and dispose of contaminated water safely. A sewage cleanup crew's job starts where theirs stops: containment, extraction, demolition of affected materials, and disinfection.

## What Containment, PPE, and Disinfection Steps Should a Crew Follow?

A crew handling a sewage backup should contain the affected area, wear full personal protective equipment, and disinfect every surface the water touched before drying begins. Skipping any of these steps leaves contamination behind even if the floor looks dry.

Containment means sealing off the affected room with poly sheeting so contaminated air and debris don't spread to the rest of the home. PPE for Category 3 water typically includes a Tyvek suit, nitrile gloves, boot covers, and a respirator, not just rubber gloves and a mask. Once standing water is extracted, the crew should apply an EPA-registered antimicrobial to hard surfaces and remove porous materials that can't be reliably disinfected. Because [mold can begin growing on wet materials within 24 to 48 hours](https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-cleanup-your-home), the containment and drying phase has to move fast, and air scrubbers or HEPA filtration should run throughout to manage airborne particulates during demolition.

## What Certifications and Training Should You Ask About?

Ask whether the technicians arriving at your home have training specific to water damage and sanitation, since that's the industry standard for how Category 3 losses should be handled. The [IICRC](https://www.iicrc.org/) sets the S500 water restoration standard that governs categorization, containment, and drying documentation across the restoration industry.

You don't need to memorize the standard yourself. You just need to ask the company directly: "Are your technicians trained to the S500 standard for Category 3 water?" A company that can answer specifically, rather than vaguely gesturing at "we're certified," is one that actually trains to it. Also ask who is physically on-site running the job, not just answering the phone, since sewage cleanup is a hands-on, same-day process, not something scheduled a week out.

## Who Handles the Insurance Paperwork, and What Will Your Policy Actually Cover?

Most standard homeowners policies do not automatically cover sewer or drain backups, even though they cover other sudden water losses; that coverage usually requires a separate water backup (sewer/sump) endorsement added to the policy. According to the [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance), coverage for water damage depends heavily on the source and whether specific endorsements were purchased, so it's worth calling your agent before assuming a backup is covered.

Because coverage is conditional, documentation matters more here than on a typical burst-pipe claim. A good sewage cleanup company photographs the affected area before demolition, logs moisture readings throughout drying, and provides a written scope of work your adjuster can review. Ask upfront who writes that documentation and whether it's included in the cleanup cost or billed separately. If the company can't describe its documentation process clearly, that's a sign the claim paperwork will land on you.
