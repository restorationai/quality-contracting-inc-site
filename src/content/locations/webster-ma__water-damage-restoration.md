## Why Webster Properties See Water Damage Issues

Webster's history as a Worcester County mill town left behind a cluster of older multi-family buildings with plumbing that has been patched and repiped in stages over decades, which means a single leak can travel through balloon-frame wall cavities before it ever shows up as a stain on a ceiling. Properties closer to the French River and Webster Lake (Lake Chaubunagungamaug) also deal with a higher water table, so basements and crawlspaces are more prone to seepage after heavy rain or fast snowmelt. Add a New England winter freeze-thaw cycle, where pipes in uninsulated exterior walls or unheated additions can freeze overnight and split by midday, and you get a steady pattern of water losses that have more to do with the building's age and site than with any single bad storm.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Webster

We start the same way on every job: find the source, stop it if it's still active, and get standing water out with truck-mount or portable extraction before it migrates further into subfloor and framing. In Webster's older homes, that often means pulling baseboards and checking plaster and lath separately from drywall sections, since plaster absorbs water slowly but releases it even more slowly, which changes how long drying equipment needs to stay in place. We set air movers and dehumidifiers based on moisture readings taken with a penetrating meter, not guesswork, and we document readings daily so the drying plan, and the insurance file, reflects what the materials are actually doing rather than how long it's been since the loss. Structural drying on a mill-era home with original subfloor can run longer than a comparable job in a newer build, and we tell homeowners that up front instead of letting the equipment sit on an optimistic timeline.
