Finishing a renovation or new build in Franklin Town doesn't end when the last nail is driven. Drywall dust settles into window tracks, sawdust works its way under baseboards, and sticky residue from tape and adhesive clings to flooring long after the crew packs up. Post-construction and specialty cleaning closes that gap, turning a job site back into a livable space before move-in day or a final building department walkthrough.

## Why Franklin Town Properties See Post-Construction Cleaning Needs

Franklin Town's building stock runs in two directions: older farmhouse-era homes that have been added onto or gutted room by room, and newer subdivision construction built over the last couple of decades. Both generate different cleanup problems. Older homes often have plaster or lath-and-plaster sections mixed with new drywall, and that combination throws off a finer, more persistent dust than drywall alone. Newer builds tend to generate more packaging debris, shrink-wrap, adhesive overspray, and fine particulate from HVAC installation that gets pulled through the duct system and redistributed room to room if it's not addressed before the system runs continuously.

## Our Post-Construction and Specialty Cleaning Process in Franklin Town

We start with bulk debris and packaging removal so flooring crews and painters aren't tracking material residue into finished rooms. From there, dust abatement comes before surface cleaning, since wiping down a countertop before the air is settled just means re-cleaning it an hour later. We work top to bottom: light fixtures, ceiling fans, and crown molding first, then walls and window tracks, then flooring and baseboards last. Cabinetry interiors, appliance surfaces, and bathroom fixtures get a detail pass separate from the general clean, since adhesive residue and paint overspray usually need a different approach than everyday dust. For projects tied to a certificate of occupancy, we time the final clean so it lines up with the building department's walkthrough schedule rather than finishing early and having dust resettle before inspection.

## Local note

A meaningful share of Franklin Town's older properties, especially those outside the denser village center, still run on private wells and septic rather than municipal water and sewer. That changes how we handle a final clean: we're careful about where rinse water and cleaning solution drain, and we avoid letting construction dust settle near well caps or septic access points during exterior cleanup. It's a small detail, but it matters on a town where municipal and private utility service sit side by side block by block.

## What Makes Specialty Cleaning Different from a Standard Clean

A standard post-construction clean handles dust, debris, and surface residue. Specialty cleaning covers the parts of a project that don't fit that category: stripping protective film from new windows without scratching low-E coatings, degreasing range hoods and appliance interiors before a first use, polishing hardwood or tile that's been under paper and plastic for weeks, and pulling adhesive residue off cabinet hardware and fixtures without damaging the finish. These aren't steps a general cleaning crew is set up for, and skipping them usually shows up as a punch-list item during a final walkthrough with the contractor or the homeowner.
