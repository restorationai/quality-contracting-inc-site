## Why Milford Properties See Storm Damage

A lot of the storm damage we're called out for in Milford isn't the dramatic kind. It's water that enters through a lifted shingle or a gutter torn loose by ice or wind, then travels along roof sheathing before it ever shows up as a ceiling stain. Older homes with slate or asphalt roofs nearing the end of their service life are especially vulnerable once a storm compromises even one section. Mature trees, a defining feature of many Milford yards, also mean limb strikes on roofs and siding are routine after high-wind events, and a cracked gutter or dented downspout can quietly redirect water toward a foundation for weeks before anyone notices.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Milford

We start with a roof and exterior assessment to identify every point water could be entering, not just the obvious one. On older Milford homes, that often means checking original flashing details around chimneys and dormers that may have been patched more than once over the decades. Any active water intrusion gets tarped or boarded to stop further loss, then we move inside to extract standing water and set up drying equipment calibrated to the building materials involved. Plaster and older lath construction, still common in Milford's historic neighborhoods, hold moisture longer than modern drywall, so we adjust drying timelines and monitor with moisture meters rather than guessing based on a calendar.

Once the structure is stabilized, we document everything with photos and moisture readings for your insurance claim, then move into repair: roofing, siding, drywall, and any structural carpentry a tree strike or wind event required. Reconstruction is handled by the same team, so there's no handoff between a mitigation company and a separate contractor.
