## Why Wellesley Properties See Carpet Issues

Wellesley's housing stock skews older than many surrounding towns, with a meaningful share of homes built in the early-to-mid twentieth century and still carrying original wool or wool-blend carpet in stairwells, hallways, and formal living spaces. Wool reacts differently to cleaning chemistry and moisture than the synthetic broadloom installed in newer builds, and using the wrong extraction settings on it can over-wet the backing and cause shrinkage or browning. New England winters add another layer: salt and sand tracked in off driveways and sidewalks grind into carpet pile near entryways and mudrooms, and homes with finished basements, common for playrooms and home offices, often have wall-to-wall carpet laid directly over slab, which wicks ambient moisture and holds odor longer than carpet over a wood subfloor. Summer humidity slows drying further, which is why timing and airflow matter as much as the cleaning solution itself.

## Our Carpet Cleaning Process in Wellesley

We start with a walk-through to identify fiber type, age, and problem areas, since a wool stair runner and a polyester family-room carpet get different treatment plans even in the same house. Traffic lanes and spot stains are pre-treated first, then we run hot water extraction at a pressure and temperature calibrated to the fiber, followed by a neutral pH rinse so no detergent residue is left behind to re-attract soil. Carpet is groomed to lift the pile, and we set up air movers to push drying time down, which matters more in Wellesley's humid summer stretches than it does in drier months. Commercial spaces, including office suites and common areas in multi-tenant buildings, typically get scheduled after hours or during low-occupancy windows so cleaning doesn't disrupt a workday.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Carpet

For most residential carpet we use truck-mounted hot water extraction, which pulls more moisture out of the pile than portable units and shortens overall drying time. Wool and delicate fiber areas get a lower-moisture approach, sometimes encapsulation rather than full extraction, to protect the backing. Stain-specific treatments, including enzyme-based solutions for pet accidents and oxidizing agents for tannin stains like wine or coffee, are applied before the general cleaning pass rather than after, since pre-treatment gives the chemistry time to break down the stain before extraction.
