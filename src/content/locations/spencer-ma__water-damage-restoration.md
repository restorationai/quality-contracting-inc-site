## Why Spencer Properties See Water Damage Issues

Spencer sits inland in Worcester County, which means winters bring hard freeze-thaw cycles that stress old galvanized supply lines and cast iron drain pipes long before anyone sees a drip. A lot of the housing stock here dates to the late 1800s and early 1900s, with fieldstone or brick foundations that were never meant to handle modern basement finishing or sump pump loads. When those foundations take on groundwater after spring thaw or a heavy nor'easter, the moisture often shows up as a damp smell or efflorescence on the stone long before standing water is visible. Newer homes on the edges of town built on slab or with modern poured foundations handle bulk water differently, but they're not immune, especially where PVC supply lines were installed without proper support and start to sag or crack at the joints over fifteen to twenty years.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Spencer

The first call is about stopping the source and getting extraction equipment running, whether that's a failed washing machine supply line, a sump pump that couldn't keep up, or a pipe that split overnight while the heat was off during a cold snap. We pull standing water with truck-mount extraction where access allows, then move to portable units for finished basements or additions where a truck can't reach the affected rooms. Older Spencer homes with plaster-and-lath walls hold moisture differently than drywall: the surface can read dry on a moisture meter while the lath behind it is still saturated, so we check at multiple depths rather than trusting one reading. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard for water mitigation, with air movers and dehumidifiers placed and monitored daily until the readings confirm the structure is actually dry, not just dry to the touch. For homes with fieldstone basement walls, we also watch for continued wicking from the foundation itself, since stone can keep feeding moisture back into framing for days after the original water event is gone.
