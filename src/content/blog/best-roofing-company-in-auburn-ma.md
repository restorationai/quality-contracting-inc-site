## What Should You Ask Before Hiring a Roofing Contractor in Auburn?

Ask for proof of license and insurance, a written scope of work, and how the contractor handles the insurance claim if the job is storm-related. Massachusetts roofing work is governed by the state building code, which follows the International Residential Code for things like underlayment, ice-and-water-shield placement, and ventilation; a contractor who can speak to [code requirements](https://www.iccsafe.org/) specifically, not just "we'll match what's there," is usually the more experienced option.

Also ask what happens if the crew finds decking rot or water damage once the old shingles come off. That's common on older Central Massachusetts homes, and it changes both the cost and the timeline. A contractor who can handle that discovery directly, rather than stopping the job to bring in a separate water damage company, saves weeks.

## Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Roof Damage?

Most standard homeowners policies cover roof damage from sudden events like wind, hail, or a falling tree limb, but not damage from gradual wear, age, or poor maintenance. The [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/which-disasters-are-covered-by-homeowners-insurance) notes that coverage depends on the cause of loss, which is why documenting the damage right after a storm, with photos and a written contractor assessment, matters for the claim. QCI documents roof and interior damage together when both are affected by the same event, which keeps the adjuster's file consistent.

If you're not sure whether your situation is a roofing call or a broader storm-damage claim, the mitigation steps and insurance sequencing are the same either way: stop further water intrusion first, document everything, then get a written scope before repairs begin.
